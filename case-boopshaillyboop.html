BoopShaillyBoop

An art mail club. One envelope a month, made by hand, sent from India.

YEAR2026, ongoing

01

I designed it

I have made things with my hands for as long as I can remember. Four years of putting creativity on deadlines and screens slowly wore that instinct down.

BoopShaillyBoop is the impulse to make things again, turned into something I can send.

Everything arrives instantly now, and most of it means nothing.

A physical envelope is proof that someone sat down, spent their time, and made something slowly, by hand, for you. That's the whole product.

The first batch is capped at 30.

If it got big too fast, the old pressure to be perfect would come straight back, and that's the thing I built this to get away from.

Every envelope in a batch is identical.

Everyone gets the same thing in the same week. That shared experience turns a list of subscribers into a club.

Everything weighs under 50g.

The postal weight limit sets the shipping cost, which sets the price, which decides who can afford to join.

One member's artwork goes into everyone's envelope.

I wanted a place where art finds an audience even when the person who made it doubts it.

02

I built it, warts and all

I don't write code from scratch. I work with AI tools, and I know enough to specify what I need, test it, and debug it when it breaks.

I built the site in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, deployed it from GitHub, and connected live Razorpay checkout. The member form writes to a Google Sheet and generates my shipping list, and the accounting app keeps the club running.

People get cold feet about automatic recurring payments.

Razorpay's subscription buttons didn't match the site, so I built checkout pages that sit in between. I also wanted people to see their first envelope before committing to a year. Recurring payment became a simple choice: leave it on and forget about payments, or turn it off and pay each time.

03

Boop Ledger

Subscription cash and subscription revenue are different things.

When someone pays ₹4,800 for a year, only ₹400 is earned this month. The remaining ₹4,400 is still owed in envelopes. So I built the club around double-entry bookkeeping. The system lives on my phone, logs an expense in ten seconds, and stays accurate as the club grows.

"Not yet yours" is money already collected for letters I still owe.

I built this for people my own age, and I was wrong about that.

The largest group signing up has been people between 40 and 60, who grew up writing letters and already understood the weight of a stamped envelope.

It's too early to say what I do about that. One envelope has shipped. I've noticed the pattern and I'm not going to pretend I've drawn the right conclusion from it yet, but it's the kind of thing that changes who you make each month's theme for, and I expect it will.

A hundred members in a year, and a club where people look forward to each other's art as much as mine.

And honestly, staying in love with making things and getting to hand that feeling to other people. If it keeps doing that, it's working.
