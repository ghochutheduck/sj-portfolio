The Craft

Saharanpur's wood carving industry is losing its market.

WHYMass-produced alternatives, no unified brand, and packaging that doesn't meet retail or export standards.
WHOSecond- and third-generation artisan families on Abdul Salam Road, many now discouraging their own children from the trade.

400 years old, GI-tagged, Mughal-influenced. Sheesham, Mango and Teak; jaali lattice work and Tarkashi brass inlay.

The Strategy

SANAT is a heritage-rooted, artisan-first brand that brings Saharanpur's iconic woodcarving to life.

Sustainable packaging, minimal yet culturally rich branding, and interactive storytelling tools that connect the maker to the modern conscious consumer, both in India and globally.

03

The Solution

SA (Saharanpur) + NAT (Sannat) = SANAT.

SA reflects the place of origin, Saharanpur, the historic heartland of wood carving in India. NAT is derived from Sannat (Urdu: صنعت), meaning craft, skill and trade, representing the artisan legacy passed down from Ustaad to Shaagird.

Hindi / SanskritAssociated with Brahma, the creator.
LatinMeans “heals.”
FinnishMeans “words.”

Names explored and set aside before SANAT.

NAKKASHIA direct reference to carving/detailing. Beautifully rooted, but reads as a descriptor, not a brand.
HAATHI MERE SAATHINostalgic, culturally familiar. Emotionally rich, but felt more like a collection name.
MEHRAABMughal architecture, arched motifs. Elegant, but suited to a product range, not a master brand.
TRUNK TALESNarrative-driven and quirky. Strong retail recall, but didn't capture the depth of artisan skill.
SANNATUrdu for craft, skill, trade. Deeply rooted, but needs a strong concept, as the meaning alone is vague.
AIRAAVATThe mythological elephant of the gods. Powerful symbolism, but too specific for a broad identity.

Why an elephant.

Elephants have long symbolized power, wisdom and auspiciousness in Indian culture, and artisans in Saharanpur already carve them paired with jaalis and floral motifs. Elephant statues are among the best-selling, most recognizable product categories in the region's woodcraft.

Native to the craft's own vocabulary, not decoration added on top.

On Abdul Salam Road, one building is the shop, the workshop and the home.

Ground floor: the store front, directly on the street. First floor: the workshop and godown. Upper floors: living spaces. Multiple generations live together and the skills are passed down.

Craft, commerce and community coexist in one structure. The packaging pays homage to that way of life, floor for floor.

04

Design Process

What I looked for in a name.

RootedIn the craft and artisan culture of Saharanpur.
LegibleEasy to recall, pronounce and apply visually.
RespectfulCulturally respectful and linguistically meaningful.
FlexibleSuitable for government and global platforms, and open to sub-lines.

Three packaging structures, tied to three real price tiers, from interviews with ODOP officials: low-cost for the public, mid-cost for retail, high-cost for corporate gifting. One was built.

Concept I Four compartments in a row, drawn out by a latch. The elephants sit in cavities so each lifts free cleanly.
Concept II Four drawers pulling out of a cross, with a transparent top so the whole set stays visible while it is closed.
Concept III · Built Four boxes that stack into a tapering tower with a carry handle. The one that became the building.

Every concept was made before it was judged.

The drawer concept worked on paper and in card. It was the tower that carried the idea of the building, so that is the one that went forward.

05

It was built. Four boxes, a handle, and a building you can carry home.
